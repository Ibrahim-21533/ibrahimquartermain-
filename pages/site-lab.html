02 / SITE LAB

Read the site before designing it.

Architecture does not exist in isolation. The Site Lab helps you understand the physical, environmental and human conditions that shape a place.

01

Every site has a story.

A site contains information about movement, climate, people, views, noise, vegetation, neighbouring buildings and many other conditions. Good architecture responds to these conditions.

SITE ANALYSIS

Investigate the conditions.

01

Sun

Study the movement of the sun and understand how solar exposure can influence orientation, openings and outdoor spaces.

02

Wind

Identify prevailing wind directions and consider how natural ventilation can influence building placement.

03

Access

Analyse pedestrian movement, vehicles, entrances, roads and important arrival points.

04

Noise

Identify noisy and quiet areas and determine how different functions should respond to them.

05

Views

Identify important views, attractive directions, unwanted views and opportunities for framing.

06

Context

Understand surrounding buildings, streets, vegetation, activities, culture and urban patterns.

SITE THINKING

Information becomes design direction.

01

Observe

Record what exists on and around the site without immediately judging it.

02

Map

Translate observations into diagrams, maps and visual information.

03

Identify

Find opportunities, constraints, conflicts and important relationships.

04

Respond

Allow the site analysis to influence orientation, access, form and spatial organisation.

THE KEY QUESTION

What is the site telling you?

Site analysis should not become a collection of diagrams that are never used. Every observation should have the potential to influence a design decision.

NEXT STEP

Turn site conditions into building decisions.

Explore how structure, materials, space and environmental strategies can respond to the site.
