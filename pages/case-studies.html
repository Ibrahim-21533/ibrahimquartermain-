05 / CASE STUDIES

Study buildings through their decisions.

Case studies turn existing architecture into lessons. Examine the site, concept, structure, materials, movement and experience behind real buildings.

01

Don't just look at the building.

Ask why it became what it is. A useful case study investigates the decisions that shaped the building, not simply its appearance.

CASE STUDY METHOD

Read architecture as evidence.

01

Context

Understand where the building is located and the conditions surrounding it.

02

Brief

Identify the purpose, users and requirements that the building needed to satisfy.

03

Concept

Investigate the central architectural idea that shaped the project.

04

Form

Examine how the concept became massing, geometry and architectural form.

05

Systems

Study structure, materials, environmental strategies and construction.

06

Experience

Understand how people approach, enter, move through and occupy the building.

STUDY CATEGORIES

Different buildings. Different lessons.

01

Libraries

Study how learning, concentration, social interaction, light and circulation shape library architecture.

02

Housing

Explore privacy, community, density, domestic activities and relationships between spaces.

03

Public Buildings

Examine how public architecture creates identity, access, gathering spaces and civic experience.

04

Educational Buildings

Investigate how classrooms, collaboration, movement and learning influence architectural form.

05

Cultural Buildings

Study the relationship between architecture, culture, memory, identity and public experience.

06

Sustainable Architecture

Examine passive strategies, materials, energy, climate response and environmental responsibility.

THE ARCHITECT'S EYE

What can this building teach your next project?

The purpose of a case study is not to copy architecture. It is to understand principles, decisions and relationships that can inform your own design thinking.
